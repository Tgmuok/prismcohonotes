>[!notes]+ Def 21.1
>
> A morphism of Huber pairs $(A, A^+) \to (B, B^+)$ is a continuous map $A \to B$ such that the image of $A^+$ is contained in $B^+$.

>[!abstract]+ Prop 21.2
>
> Spa is a contravariant functor: any morphism of Huber pairs $(A, A^+) \to (B, B^+)$ determines a continuous map $\operatorname{Spa}(B, B^+) \to \operatorname{Spa}(A, A^+)$.

>[!abstract]+ Prop 21.3
>
> For any Huber pair, we can form the completion $(\widehat{A}, \widehat{A}^+)$. The induced morphism $\operatorname{Spa}(\widehat{A}, \widehat{A}^+) \to \operatorname{Spa}(A, A^+)$ is a homeomorphism.

**Remark 21.4**. As you might expect, the completion of a Huber ring is complete. It is important that the ideal of definition is required to be finite—the completion of a ring with respect to an infinitely generated ideal need not be complete!

>[!abstract]+ Prop 21.5
>
> Let $(A, A^+)$ be a complete Huber pair.
> 1. If $A \neq 0$, then $\operatorname{Spa}(A, A^+)$ is nonempty.
> 2. One has $A^+ = \{f \in A \mid |f(x)| \leq 1 \ \forall x \in X\}$.
> 3. An element $f \in A$ is invertible iff for all $x \in X$, $|f(x)| \neq 0$.

>[!notes]+ Def 21.6
>
> A rational subset of $\operatorname{Spa}(A, A^+)$ is a subset defined by inequalities $|a_1|, |a_2|, \dots, |a_n| \leq |a_0| \neq 0$, where $a_0, a_1, \dots, a_n \in A$ generate an open ideal.

**Remark 21.7**. It is important that $a_0, \dots, a_n$ generate an open ideal of $A$. Choose a ring of definition $A_0$ and ideal of definition $I$. In order for multiplication by $1/a_0$ to be continuous, we need $\frac{1}{a_0} I^n \subset A_0[a_1/a_0, \dots, a_n/a_0]$ for some $n$. It suffices to show that $I^n \subset (a_0, \dots, a_n) \cdot A_0$ for large enough $n$, i.e. we need the $A_0$-submodule of $A$ generated by $a_0, \dots, a_n$ be open. One can show that this is equivalent to $a_0, \dots, a_n$ generating an open ideal of $A$.

>[!example]+ ex 21.8
>
> If $a_0, \dots, a_n$ do not generate an open ideal, then the locus $|a_1|, \dots, |a_n| \leq |a_0| \neq 0$ may not be quasicompact, which would imply that it cannot be of the form $\operatorname{Spa}(A, A^+)$. For example, if $X = \operatorname{Spa}(\mathbb{Q}_p \langle T \rangle, \mathbb{Z}_p \langle T \rangle)$ is the closed unit disc, then the locus $|T| \neq 0$ is the punctured disc, which is not quasicompact.

>[!tip]+ lemma 21.9
>
> Rational subsets form a basis for the topology of $\operatorname{Spa}(A, A^+)$.
>>*Proof.* An intersection of two rational subsets is rational:
>>
>>$$
>>X \left( \frac{T}{a_0} \right) \cap X \left( \frac{S}{b_0} \right) = X \left( \frac{Tb_0 \cup a_0 S \cup TS}{a_0 b_0} \right).
>>$$
>>
>>It remains to show that for any $f,g \in A$, the open subset $U$ defined by $|f| \leq |g| \neq 0$ can be covered by rational subsets. Let $a_1, \dots, a_n$ be generators of an ideal of definition of some ring of definition $A_0$ of $A$. For $r \in \mathbb{N}$, let $T_r = \{f, a_1^r, \dots, a_n^r\}$. Since $a_1^r, \dots, a_n^r$ generate an open ideal of $A_0$, they also generate an open ideal of $A$. Hence the $X \left( \frac{T_r}{g} \right)$ are rational subsets of $X$ that are contained in $U$. We claim that they cover $U$. Indeed, let $x \in U$. Since $|g(x)| \neq 0$ and $\lim_{r \to \infty} a_i^r = 0$ for each $i$, we can choose $r$ so that $|a_i^r(x)| \leq |g(x)|$ for all $i$. Then $x \in X \left( \frac{T_r}{g} \right)$.

>[!abstract]+ Prop 21.10
>
> Let $X = \operatorname{Spa}(A, A^+)$, and let $U$ be a rational subset of $X$. There exists a complete Huber pair $(B, B^+)$ over $(A, A^+)$ such that $\operatorname{Spa}(B, B^+) \to \operatorname{Spa}(A, A^+)$ factors through $U$, and is universal for such maps. Moreover, the map $\operatorname{Spa}(B, B^+) \to U$ is a homeomorphism.
>
> We construct $(B, B^+)$ as follows. Choose a ring of definition $A_0$ and an ideal of definition $I$.
>
> Let the rational subset be defined by $0 \neq |a_0| \geq |a_1|, \dots, |a_n|$. Define a topology on $A[1/a_0]$ by making $A_0[a_1/a_0, \dots, a_n/a_0]$ a ring of definition with ideal of definition $I A_0[a_1/a_0, \dots, a_n/a_0]$. Then $B$ is the completion of $A[1/a_0]$, and $B^+$ is the completion of the integral closure of $A^+[a_1/a_0, \dots, a_n/a_0]$ in $A[1/s]$.

>[!notes]+ Def 21.11
>
> For any Huber pair $(A, A^+)$, we define a presheaf of topological rings $\mathcal{O}_X$ on $X = \operatorname{Spa}(A, A^+)$ as follows. If $U$ is rational, we define $\mathcal{O}_X(U)$ to be the ring $B$ of Proposition 21.10. For general $U$, we define
>
>$$
>\mathcal{O}_X(U) = \varprojlim_{W \subset U \text{ rational}} \mathcal{O}_X(W).
>$$
>
> We define $\mathcal{O}_X^+(U)$ similarly.
>
> This presheaf $\mathcal{O}_X$ is not a sheaf in general. If $\mathcal{O}_X$ is a sheaf, then $\mathcal{O}_X^+$ is also a sheaf. There are a few general classes of Huber rings that are known to be sheafy.

>[!notes]+ Def 21.12
>
> A Huber ring $A$ is Tate if it has a topologically nilpotent unit.

>[!notes]+ Def 21.13
>
> A Huber ring $A$ is strongly Noetherian if $A \langle X_1, \dots, X_n \rangle$ is Noetherian for each $n$. Here
>
>$$
>A \langle X_1, \dots, X_n \rangle = \left\{ \sum_{\underline{i} \in \mathbb{N}^n} a_{\underline{i}} X^{\underline{i}} \bigg| \lim_{\underline{i} \to \infty} a_{\underline{i}} = 0 \right\}
>$$

>[!notes]+ Def 21.14
>
> A Huber ring $A$ is uniform if $A^\circ$ is bounded (equivalently, $A^\circ$ is a ring of definition). The pair $(A, A^+)$ is stably uniform if for every rational subset $U \subset X = \operatorname{Spa}(A, A^+)$, $\mathcal{O}_X(U)$ is uniform.

>[!tip]+ lemma 21.15
>
> The Huber pair $(A, A^+)$ is sheafy if any of the following conditions is satisfied:
> 1. $A$ is discrete. (schemes)
> 2. $A$ is finitely generated over a Noetherian ring of definition. (Noetherian formal schemes, rigid spaces over discretely valued fields)
> 3. $A$ is Tate and strongly Noetherian (rigid spaces)
> 4. $A$ is Tate and $(A, A^+)$ is stably uniform. (reduced rigid spaces, perfectoid spaces)

>[!tip]+ lemma 21.16
>
> The completion $(A, A^+) \to (\widehat{A}, \widehat{A}^+)$ induces a canonical isomorphism $\operatorname{Spa}(\widehat{A}, \widehat{A}^+) \to \operatorname{Spa}(A, A^+)$.
>
> Let $X = \operatorname{Spa}(A, A^+)$. Then $\mathcal{O}_X(X) \cong \widehat{A}$, $\mathcal{O}_X^+(X) \cong \widehat{A}^+$.

>[!notes]+ Def 21.17
>
> A $v$-ringed space is a triple $(X, \mathcal{O}_X, (|\cdot(x)|)_{x \in X})$ where $X$ is a topological space, $\mathcal{O}_X$ is a sheaf of topological rings on $X$, and for each $x \in X$, $|\cdot(x)|$ is an equivalence class of continuous valuations on $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$. A morphism of $v$-ringed spaces is a a morphism of ringed spaces, such that the maps on sections are continuous, and valuations are preserved.

>[!notes]+ Def 21.18
>
> An affinoid adic space is a $v$-ringed space that is isomorphic to some $\operatorname{Spa}(A, A^+)$. (In particular, we require that the structure presheaf is a sheaf.)
>
> An adic space is a $v$-ringed space that has an open covering by affinoid adic spaces.

**Remark 21.19**. Rational subsets of $\operatorname{Cont}(A)$ are generally not of the form $\operatorname{Cont}(B)$, and rational subsets of $\operatorname{Spa}(A, A^\circ)$ are generally not of the form $\operatorname{Spa}(B, B^\circ)$. This is why we do not try to build the theory just out of spaces of the form $\operatorname{Cont}(A)$, or spaces of the form $\operatorname{Spa}(A, A^\circ)$.
