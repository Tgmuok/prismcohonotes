Let $C = \widehat{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}_p$, and let $\mathcal{O}_C$ be its ring of integers. Let $X$ be a smooth formal scheme over $\mathcal{O}_C$. Then one can consider the following $p$-adic cohomology theories:
- The de Rham cohomology $H^i_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/\mathcal{O}_C)$;
- The étale cohomology of the generic fiber $H^i_{\mathrm{ét}}(X_C, \mathbb{Q}_p)$;
- The crystalline cohomology of the special fiber $H^i_{\mathrm{cris}}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p}/W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p))$.

Prismatic cohomology is a "universal" cohomology theory that specializes to all three of the above cohomologies.

Recall that we defined a map
$$
\Theta: A_{\mathrm{inf}} = W(\mathcal{O}_C^\flat) \to \mathcal{O}_C.
$$

The pair $(A_{\mathrm{inf}}, \ker \Theta)$ is an example of a (perfect and bounded) prism. We will define prisms later in the lecture. For now, you should just know that a prism consists of a pair $(A,I)$, where $A$ is a ring with additional structure and $I$ is an invertible ideal satisfying certain properties. In particular, the additional structure includes a map $\phi_A: A \to A$ lifting the Frobenius map $A/p \to A/p$.

Given a bounded prism $(A,I)$ and a smooth formal scheme $X$ over $A/I$, one can define the prismatic site $((X/A)_\Delta, \mathcal{O}_\Delta)$. One can then define the prismatic cohomology
$$
R\Gamma_\Delta(X/A) := R\Gamma((X/A)_\Delta, \mathcal{O}_\Delta).
$$

It is an object in the derived category of $A$-modules, and it is equipped with a $\phi_A$-linear map $\phi$.

>[!danger]+ Them 29.1
>
> (1) (Crystalline comparison) If $I = (p)$, then there is a canonical $\phi$-equivariant isomorphism
> $$
> R\Gamma_{\mathrm{cris}}(X/A) \cong \phi_A^* R\Gamma_\Delta(X/A).
> $$
> 
> (2) (de Rham comparison) There is a canonical isomorphism
> $$
> R\Gamma_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/(A/I)) \cong R\Gamma_\Delta(X/A) \hat{\otimes}_{A, \phi_A}^L A/I.
> $$
> 
> (3) (Étale comparison) Assume $A$ is perfect. Let $X_\eta$ be the generic fiber of $X$. For any $n \geq 0$, there is a canonical isomorphism
> $$
> R\Gamma_{\mathrm{ét}}(X_\eta, \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}) \cong \left( R\Gamma_\Delta(X/A)/p^n[1/I] \right)^{\phi=1}.
> $$
> 
> (4) (Base change) Let $(A,I) \to (B,J)$ be a map of bounded prisms, and let $Y = X \times_{\mathrm{Spf}(A/I)} \mathrm{Spf}(B,J)$. Then there is a canonical isomorphism
> $$
> R\Gamma_\Delta(X/A) \hat{\otimes}_A^L B \cong R\Gamma_\Delta(Y/B).
> $$


**Remark 29.2.** In particular, (4) can be applied with $A = W(\mathcal{O}_{K^\flat})$, $I = \ker \Theta$, $B = W(k)$, $I = (p)$, where $k$ is the residue field of $K$. Combined with (1), we obtain a comparison of the prismatic cohomology of $X$ and with the crystalline cohomology of its special fiber.

>[!example]+ ex 29.3
>
> Let $K$ be a perfectoid field. Let $k$ be the residue field of $K$. Suppose $A = W(\mathcal{O}_{K^\flat})$, $I = \ker \Theta$, $X = \mathrm{Spf}(A/I) = \mathrm{Spf}(\mathcal{O}_K)$. Then $R\Gamma_\Delta(X/A)$ is just $A$ in degree 0.
> 
> If $\mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathrm{cyc}} \subseteq K$, define
> $$
> A\{1\} := \phi_A^{-1}(\mu) A.
> $$
> where $\mu = [\epsilon] - 1$. (To make this construction functorial, we should tensor with $\mathbb{Z}_p(-1)$, but for simplicity, we will ignore this issue.) We will compare the crystalline, étale, and de Rham specializations of $A$ and $A\{1\}$. We may as well take the Frobenius twist now; we have $\phi_A^* A \cong A$, $\phi_A^* A\{1\} \cong \mu A$.
> 
> The crystalline specialization of $A$ is $W(k)$ with the usual Frobenius action. The crysatlline specialization of $A\{1\}$ is a free $W(k)$-module of rank 1 generated by $\mu$. The Frobenius sends $\mu \mapsto p\mu$ since the image of $\phi(\mu)/\mu = \frac{[\epsilon]^p - 1}{[\epsilon]-1} = 1+[\epsilon]+\dots+[\epsilon]^{p-1}$ in $W(k)$ is $p$.
> 
> The étale specialization of $A$ is
> $$
> \left( A \otimes_A W(K^\flat)/p^n \right)^{\phi=1} \cong W(\mathbb{F}_p)/p^n \cong \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}.
> $$
> Since $\epsilon -1 \neq 0$, $\mu$ is a unit in $W(K^\flat)$, we again get $\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$ for the étale specialization.
> 
> The de Rham specialization in each case is a free $\mathcal{O}_C$-module of rank 1 We recover the Hodge filtration as follows. We have
> $$
> A \otimes_A B_{\mathrm{dR}} \cong A\{1\} \otimes_A B_{\mathrm{dR}} \cong \mathbb{Z}_p \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} B_{\mathrm{dR}}
> $$
> where $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is the inverse limit of the étale specializations. We take the usual filtration on $\mathbb{Z}_p \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ and pull it back to $A \otimes_A B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$ and $A\{1\} \otimes_A B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. We see that $A \otimes_A B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ = \mathrm{Fil}^0$ and $A\{1\} \otimes_A B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ = \mu B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+ = \mathrm{Fil}^1$.

Let $A$ be a ring. Let $\phi: A \to A$ be an endomorphism of $A$ lifting the Frobenius on $A/pA$. Then $\phi(x) = x^p + p\delta(x)$ for some function $\delta: A \to A$. An arbitrariy chosen $\delta$ will not necessarily give us an endomorphism of $A$. If we impose the following constraints on $\delta$, then we will get an endomorphism.

$$
\delta(0) = \delta(1) = 0 \tag{29.4}
$$

$$
\delta(x + y) = \delta(x) + \delta(y) - \sum_{n=1}^{p-1} \frac{(p-1)!}{n!(p-n)!} x^n y^{p-n} \tag{29.5}
$$

$$
\delta(xy) = y^p \delta(x) + x^p \delta(y) + p \delta(x) \delta(y) \tag{29.6}
$$

>[!notes]+ Def 29.7
>
> A $\delta$-ring is a pair $(A,\delta)$ where $A$ is a commutative ring and $\delta: A \to A$ is a map of sets satisfying equations (29.4–29.6).

**Remark 29.8.** If $p$ is not a zero divisor in $A$, then $\delta$-ring structures on $A$ are in bijection with lifts of Frobenius on $A$. On the other hand, the identity $\delta(p^n) = p^{n-1} - p^{pn-1}$ shows that if $p$ is nilpotent in $A$ (and $A$ is not the zero ring), then $A$ has no $\delta$-ring structures.

>[!notes]+ Def 29.9
>
> A prism is a pair $(A,I)$ where $A$ is a $\delta$-ring and $I$ is an ideal of $A$ such that $A$ is derived $(p,I)$-complete, and $p \in I + \phi(I)A$.

We will not attempt to define what it means for a ring to be derived $(p,I)$-complete. However, "complete" implies "derived complete" and "derived complete and separated" implies "complete".

>[!notes]+ Def 29.10
>
> A prism is perfect if the Frobenius map $\phi: A \to A$ is an isomorphism.
> 
> A prism is bounded if it has bounded $p^\infty$-torsion, i.e. $A[p^\infty] = A[p^n]$ for some $n$.

>[!example]+ ex 29.11
>
> (1) As mentioned before, for any perfectoid field $K$, $(W(\mathcal{O}_{K^\flat}), \ker \Theta)$ is a prism with $A/I \cong \mathcal{O}_K$, and for any perfect field $k$, $(W(k), (p))$ is a prism with $A/I \cong k$. These are perfect and bounded.
> 
> (2) Let $A = \mathbb{Z}_p [\![ T ]\!]$ with Frobenius $T \mapsto T^p$, and let $I = (T - p)$. Then $(A,I)$ is a bounded prism with $A/I \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$.
> 
> (3) Let $A = \mathbb{Z}_p [\![ T ]\!]$ with Frobenius $T \mapsto (1+T)^p - 1$, and let $I = \left( \frac{(1+T)^p -1}{T} \right)$. Then $(A,I)$ is a bounded prism with $A/I \cong \mathbb{Z}_p[\zeta_p]$.

>[!tip]+ lemma 29.12
>
> Let $(A,I)$ be a prism, and let $J \subset I$ be an ideal of $A$ such that $(A,J)$ is a prism. Then $J = I$.

>[!done]+ Coro 29.13
>
> If $(A,I) \to (B,J)$ is a map of prisms, then $J = IB$.

>[!notes]+ Def 29.14
>
> Let $(A,I)$ be a bounded prism. Let $X$ be a smooth $p$-adic formal scheme over $A/I$. Define $(X/A)_\Delta$ to be the category of maps $(A,I) \to (B,IB)$ of bounded prisms, together with a map $\mathrm{Spf}(B/IB) \to X$ over $A/I$.
> 
> A morphism in $(X/A)_\Delta$ is a flat cover if the induced map of prisms $(B,IB) \to (C,IC)$ is faithfully flat, i.e. $C$ is $(p,IB)$-completely flat over $B$ ($C/(p,I)C$ is a flat $B/(p,I)B$-module and $\mathrm{Tor}_n^B(C, B/(p,I)B) = 0$ for $n > 0$).
> 
> The prismatic site of $X$ is the category $(X/A)_\Delta$ along with the topology defined by flat covers. Define sheaves $\mathcal{O}_\Delta, \overline{\mathcal{O}}_\Delta$ on $(X/A)_\Delta$ by
> $$
> \mathcal{O}_\Delta(\mathrm{Spf}(B) \leftarrow \mathrm{Spf}(B/IB) \to X) = B
> $$
> $$
> \overline{\mathcal{O}}_\Delta(\mathrm{Spf}(B) \leftarrow \mathrm{Spf}(B/IB) \to X) = B/IB
> $$

The category of prisms $(A,I)$ admitting a morphism $\mathcal{O}_C \to A/I$ has an initial object, namely $(A_{\mathrm{inf}}, \ker \Theta)$. However, the category of all prisms does not have an initial object. To remedy this situation, Bhatt and Lurie define a stack $\mathrm{WCart}$ such that each prism $(A,I)$ defines a morphism $\mathrm{Spf}\, A \to \mathrm{WCart}$. For any qcqs formal scheme $\mathfrak{X}$ over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ with bounded torsion, they define a complex $\mathcal{H}_\Delta(\mathfrak{X})$ on $\mathrm{WCart}$ such that the prismatic cohomology groups $R\Gamma_\Delta(X_{A/I}/A)$ are recovered by pullback.
